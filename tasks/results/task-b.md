## D0. Why hearts are missed

1. **Support and safety — “You cannot screen by risk factor,” D0-Q03 explanation, video beat 1.** IS23-080 refers to a *high proportion of prenatally detectable* disease in pregnancies without risk factors or extracardiac anomalies. It gives no proportion for babies whose defect was missed or for all affected babies, and no guarantee that a routine scan finds each one. Replace “most of those babies” and the promise of detection with that population and qualifier. For the scope of routine low-risk second-trimester screening and the recommended views cite IS23-001 and IS23-018. Distinguish routine screening from the stem’s “examining the heart in detail,” which could mean a specialist study (IS23-079).
2. **Support — objective 2, “Where the miss happens,” D0-Q04/Q05 and video beat 2.** The sourced prose correctly restricts the figures to one retrospective study. The objective and video generalise to the usual causes of all misses. Identify the study in the video and objective. In Q05, replace “in the same study” with a stand-alone stem. Drop the uncited claim that recognition is trainable or cite the narrower feedback-based training finding (IS23-088, IS23-006).
3. **Unsupported framing — “Where the miss happens” and “Why detection varies.”** “Fixed by” promises prevention. IS23-088 describes contributing failures and IS23-006 describes programme features. Say the course practises acquisition and recognition. For unfavourable fetal lie, say additional examination time or a second examination *may* be needed (IS23-056), instead of saying waiting or recall often solves it. Move this instruction into a cited block. IS23-005 supports the list of contributing factors.
4. **Safety and unsupported framing — “What this course asks of you” and video beat 4.** “Refer early when a plane fails” conflates an incomplete view with a suspected lesion. State the recommended views (IS23-018/023) and say that suspected abnormality is sufficient for echo candidacy (IS23-078). For unfavourable lie use IS23-056. The generic deadline “early” lacks a claim here. Keep “within a few beats” only as an educational exercise, never a clinical recognition standard. Beat 4 has no citations at all.
5. **Support — “Four chambers are not enough,” video beat 3 and D0-Q07.** IS23-067 says *complete* transposition is typically abnormal on the three-vessel view. It does not include coarctation. Restore “complete” in Q07. In beat 3 separately link coarctation to the usefulness of the three-vessel-and-trachea view (IS23-054/071) and retain “may” or “typically” as applicable. “Where they show” implies certain detection.
6. **Support — checklist items 1, 2 and 5.** For item 1 specify routine second-trimester screening (IS23-001/018), since the minimum early examination differs (IS23-091). IS23-007 addresses counting in the four-chamber and three-vessel views specifically. Limit item 2 to them or cite the other planes’ actual criteria. In item 5 distinguish audit of stored images against predetermined criteria from the *annual* review of detection and diagnostic accuracy. Both are encouraged, and IS23-089 specifies an annual interval only for the latter.
7. **Drills — first-option check.** Q01, Q03–Q10 have one ledger-backed best option, subject to the stem/feedback changes above. In Q02, “almost none” is logically compatible with “fewer than half” and the ledger gives no lower bound. Ask which wording IS23-004 uses or replace the distractor with “exactly half.” Q10’s other choices are all factors in IS23-005, but do not answer which is most directly within the examiner’s control. Q06 asks the pair named in IS23-008 most cleanly if the stem says so.
8. **Fetal-sex wording and source mixing.** No findings in either category. D0 cites only the screening ledger.

## D7. Colour Doppler in screening

1. **Unsupported framing — “Is colour required?”** The rules that grey-scale must come first and that colour cannot rescue a poor view are clinical assertions without citations. IS23-041 supports displaying colour beside B-mode and IS23-073 supports competent, encouraged use. Use those statements in a cited block. For a prescribed sequence or universal limitation, **MISSING CLAIM**. In video beat 1 “learn it on normal hearts first” can be explicitly presented as the course exercise.
2. **Support and source mixing — “Setting up colour,” block 3; “Power Doppler”; D7-Q11.** AS23-010 is echocardiography optimisation. Label it as such before instructing operators to adjust gain and persistence. The AS23-011 description of power Doppler is supported as a mode property, but using it for septal defects is an echo application in this evidence. Identify the context in the subsection and Q11 feedback. The clearly labelled AS23-009 specialist example under “What colour exposes” needs no change.
3. **Support and safety — settings table rows 1–2, D7-Q03, checklist items 1–5 and video beat 2.** The two table values accurately match IS23-076/077; retain their second-trimester caption when reused. The independent checklist and beat omit the second-trimester restriction, and the beat omits cm/s. Add scope and units to both and to Q03’s stem. Introduce checklist items 1–5 with “when colour is used” so findings that colour *can show* do not become mandatory acquisitions (IS23-073/074). Keep the early-scan item distinct (IS23-092/093).
4. **Support — “Always beside grey-scale” and D7-Q05 explanation.** The ledger gives the reason for the display, rather than a guarantee against false negatives. Say “display colour beside B-mode to help avoid false-negative findings” (IS23-041). The keyed purpose in Q05 can stand.
5. **Unsupported statement — D7-Q07 explanation.** IS23-074 supports the flow-reversal key, but does not establish “size and axis are grey-scale judgements” as a general exclusion of colour. End the explanation after the examples of abnormal flow. IS23-094 expressly assigns grey-scale to *early detailed* situs/axis assessment.
6. **Safety — video beat 5.** IS23-074 supports the V-sign and actual flow reversal. It gives no rule that different displayed colours prove reversal or that both arches must be the same colour. Describe the V and say colour can highlight reversal in the ductus or aortic arch. Remove the stand-alone colour test and corresponding visual implication. **MISSING CLAIM** for interpreting colour differences as proof.
7. **Source mixing — “Colour in early pregnancy” block 3 and D7-Q09.** IS23-093 specifies the screening thermal index and brief exposure. AS23-006 separately gives both thermal and mechanical indices in early echocardiography. Replace “same direction” with explicit scopes and keep IS23-093 as the screening answer citation. The values overlap, but the two requirements are not identical.
8. **Drills and fetal-sex wording.** Q01–Q11 each have one ledger-backed first option with the above scope repairs. Q02’s lower scale is supported for *veins*, not the heart and great arteries in its stem. Q08’s pulsed-wave sampling distractor is a different examination (AS23-013), not a second screening answer. Q09 asks for the recommended limit, so the higher ceilings are not alternatives to it. No fetal-sex wording found.

## D8. Rate and rhythm for the screener

1. **Unsupported framing and safety — “Normal,” summary and objective 2.** The normal range and regularity have a claim (IS23-033); add IS23-018 for the four-chamber screening view. The universal three-way “normal, watch and re-check, or refer” scheme and promise to distinguish benign ectopy in an individual extend beyond IS23-034/035. Teach recognition of the *listed triggers for further assessment*. The ledger does not assign disposition to every intermediate rate or pattern. **MISSING CLAIM** for a complete triage scheme.
2. **Support and safety — “The slow heart” block 3, “At a glance” rows 4–5, D8-Q05, checklist items 3–4, video beat 4.** IS23-036 describes transient bradycardia in normal *second-trimester* fetuses, often associated with pressure, and calls for *timely* specialist evaluation for persistent ≤110 bpm in a *well* fetus. Restore these qualifiers wherever the snippets stand alone. The separate IS23-082 lists persistent bradycardia as an echo indication without the “well” qualifier. Label that separately. Neither claim makes easing pressure and observing until recovery a sufficient clinical protocol or establishes a waiting period. Describe the observed recovery in Q05, avoid diagnosing fetal well-being from recovery, and drop the prescriptive observation rule unless sourced.
3. **Support and safety — “At a glance” row 2, checklist item 6, video beats 2–3.** IS23-034 supports “often benign,” with the *low-risk population* qualification attaching to the absence of increased structural-disease risk. Pair isolated reassurance in beat 2 with IS23-035’s frequent-ectopy/persistent-irregularity triggers. IS23-035 specifies *more than* one to two weeks. “After a week or two” is imprecise and the claim does not prescribe a recheck for every ectopic beat or a fixed interval. Use the two separate triggers verbatim in meaning, preserving the disjunction and attaching time only to persistent irregularity.
4. **Drill validity — D8-Q03 and Q04.** Q03’s “after every second or third normal beat” does not specify unambiguously how all beats are counted against “more often than every three to five beats.” Use an unequivocally frequent example such as every other beat. Q04 says two weeks elapsed but calls that “beyond” one to two weeks; the endpoints alone also leave persistence implicit. State that irregularity has persisted for longer than two weeks. Option 1 then follows directly in both items (IS23-035); no other offered option fixes the ambiguous stems.
5. **Support and source mixing — “What the specialist will do,” final framing block and D8-Q11.** AS23-016/017/018 are correctly placed in specialist rhythm evaluation. The uncited assertion that none of the techniques is needed to screen is broader than those entries. Delete that clause and keep the family explanation as course orientation. Specify a *ventricular free wall* in Q11 option 1 as in the explanation. No specialist rhythm-documentation mandate is otherwise imposed on screening.
6. **Support — video beats 1 and 5.** “Every four-chamber view” reads as a direction for every image/clip rather than an examination criterion. Say “At screening, assess rate and regularity on the four-chamber view” (IS23-018/033). In beat 5 give the actual 160–180 bpm range for the brief movement-associated rise, and say persistent ≥180 bpm warrants further evaluation (IS23-037), rather than leaving “cannot be left” unexplained.
7. **Drills and fetal-sex wording.** Q01, Q02 and Q05–Q11 each have one supported first choice. Q03 and Q04 require the repairs above before scoring them as uniquely correct. In Q05 specify second trimester in the stem and avoid the universal “persistent bradycardia is what needs evaluation” in the feedback. In Q08 transient movement-associated tachycardia *can* be normal; the ledger does not establish hypoxia in that scenario. No fetal-sex wording found.

IS23-078 identifies the latter as an echo candidate. IS23-056 says an unfavourable lie may require additional time or a second examination. A low referral threshold (IS23-006) does not itself make every technically failed image an indication. Give Q01 a technically adequate view with suspected abnormal anatomy, even though the lesion cannot be named. Use the same distinction in the checklist. **MISSING CLAIM** for a universal referral rule following failed acquisition.
2. **Support — “Risk factors add to screening” block 1, objective 1 and Q02/Q09.** “How this is arranged” makes IS23-079’s qualifications sound purely logistical. Keep the dependence of the indication on resources, setting, examiner availability and screening results in the same sentence. Q02 and Q09 can ask whether each factor *is listed* in IS23-083, preserving their keys without purporting to prescribe the entire individual decision. Use “candidate for” for an abnormal screen (IS23-078). In objective 5 change “must take into account” to “should take into account” (IS23-086).
3. **Support and safety — “Fetal indications” table rows 6 and 8, checklist items 2/5/6 and video beat 2.** Separate “suspected heart block” from “persistent bradycardia ≤110 bpm”: the threshold qualifies only the latter (IS23-082). Add suspected block to item 2. In item 5 restore *confirmed or suspected* genetic abnormality. In row 8/item 6 add the 11–14-week window from IS23-081 even if the later measurement normalises. In the video replace “thick” and “too slow or too fast” with the actual listed indications and thresholds, preserve *frequent or persistent* irregularity and *suspected* dysfunction, and label any partial enumeration as examples.
4. **Support and safety — checklist items 3–4.** For the great arteries IS23-051 says *major* discrepancy warrants further evaluation; “clearly unequal” can include the normal slight difference (IS23-065). For the ventricles IS23-043 specifies mid-gestation and *further examination*. Item 4’s findings respectively raise suspicion about situs or raise malformation risk (IS23-030/031). 5. **Support and safety — checklist item 7, video beat 3, maternal/family table.** The table’s seven rows agree with IS23-083, but the short formats omit the first-/second-degree relative with Mendelian disease and childhood cardiac manifestations, broaden PKU beyond unknown status or periconceptional phenylalanine >10 mg/dL, and imply any confirmed fetal infection. Use the full table or label the short lists as examples and direct readers to it. Include the missing fetal indications, such as monochorionic twinning *if resources allow*, in any checklist presented as complete (IS23-082).
6. **Safety and unsupported framing — summary, “Not enough evidence of raised risk,” its table and following framing, and video beat 4.** All ten individual factor entries track IS23-085, but the summary’s “which do not [lead to echo]” and “does not list them as indications” read as a negative referral decision. 7. **Support and drills — D11-Q12 and video beat 5.** “Most babies with heart disease” changes population and quantity. The answer to the current “why most” stem does not establish its premise. Ask which observation explains the importance of quality routine screening. ## D13. Quality assurance and audit

1. **Unsupported framing — “Why it matters.”** IS23-017/087/089 support archiving for later review, checking interpretation and reviewing detection/accuracy. They do not establish that follow-up by itself proves whether abnormal anatomy was recognised on the original images. Replace with the supported separate activities. **MISSING CLAIM** for an outcome-ascertainment and case-attribution method.
2. **Unsupported framing and safety — “Auditing yourself,” “The checklist” and video beats 3–4.** Monthly random samples, three scoring categories, review of every later-diagnosed case and use of the same checklist for every audit are a proposed local design, not methods in IS23-089. Remove the numerical schedule and scoring protocol from sourced teaching or retain it only as an explicitly local, unvalidated proposal outside the clinical lesson. Reviewing cases selected for later disease cannot on its own establish overall diagnostic accuracy; the ledger supplies no denominator or ascertainment method. State that preset quality criteria for stored images/clips are encouraged, as is annual review of *both* detection and diagnostic accuracy, with training where needed. Beat 3 changes encouragement to an instruction to score each plane with this exact list. Beat 4 omits diagnostic accuracy. **MISSING CLAIM** for a scoring system or a validated local accuracy calculation.
3. **Support and source mixing — “What to store” blocks 2–3, D13-Q04, video beat 2.** IS23-063’s continuum covers the RVOT, branch pulmonary arteries and ductal/aortic arches. It does not cover every “upper plane” or both outflow tracts as one continuum. Name these views, or cite IS23-058 separately for the relation of the two outflows on cine. Keep stills *and* clips in the stand-alone video per IS23-017. The ASE archival requirements and structured report are correctly labelled as belonging to full echocardiography. Keep sagittal arch clips out of the routine screening checklist (IS23-061).
4. **Support — checklist items 3, 4 and 6.** Restore *usually* to the one-third heart/chest *area* criterion (IS23-026). Name the chest anteroposterior axis as the reference for item 4 (IS23-028). Item 6 reduces a moving flap to its position: check that the foramen ovale flap moves within the left atrium (IS23-038).
5. **Safety — checklist items 7 and 8.** IS23-043 allows mild disproportion as a third-trimester variant and calls for further examination of mid-gestation asymmetry. Preserve that distinction in the audit key. IS23-044 calls for *examining* the septum apex to crux and, where possible, sweeping posteriorly toward the outflow tracts. “Septum intact” demands certainty an image may not provide: small defects may be hard to confirm and beam orientation may produce dropout (IS23-045/046). Record the assessment and its adequacy instead of requiring proof of intactness.
6. **Support and safety — five-plane checklist scope, items 2, 7, 9–13.** If this is offered as the complete screening/audit checklist, include at least one pulmonary vein entering the left atrium on B-mode *where technically feasible* (IS23-040). Preserve features that distinguish the planes: descending aorta at the abdominal circumference level (IS23-024); morphological right ventricular moderator band near the apex (IS23-042); separately opening AV valves and septal tricuspid leaflet offset (IS23-047); great-artery connections and expected branching/non-branching (IS23-049/052/059/064); and number, alignment, arrangement and size on the three-vessel view (IS23-066). Alternatively label it a selection of checks and make clear that completing it does not establish a complete normal examination.
7. **Support — checklist item 15.** “Laterality labelled” drops “where appropriate” and leaves out orientation. Archive still frames and clips of recommended structures/views, and label laterality and orientation where appropriate (IS23-017).
8. **Drills, fetal-sex wording and source mixing.** Q01–Q10 each have one ledger-backed best first option, with Q04’s anatomical scope narrowed as above. Q09’s “general obstetric preset, unchanged” is not independently specified to have the listed settings: ask which *settings* the ledger recommends, as opposed to assuming the properties of a preset. No fetal-sex wording found. No echo requirement is presented as mandatory for routine screening.

## Five findings to address first

1. **D11, “The basic rule,” Q01, checklist item 1; D0 closing section.** Teach the difference between an incomplete view and suspected abnormal anatomy. The current referral drill can be answered only by assuming those are equivalent (IS23-056/078).
2. **D11, referral checklist, insufficient-evidence table and video.** A standalone checklist drops qualifying conditions and indications, while the risk table can be read as a reason to withhold referral even after a suspicious screen. Preserve the separate indication, consideration and insufficient-evidence categories and the independent screen finding (IS23-078/079/081–085).
3. **D7, video beat 5.** Remove the rule diagnosing reversed flow solely from opposite displayed colours. Teach the actual flow finding the ledger supports (IS23-074).
4. **D8, Q03/Q04, “At a glance” and checklist.** Give unambiguous ectopy frequency and persistence examples and avoid using transient slowing or a mandatory waiting interval as a reassurance rule. Preserve the conditions for timely specialist evaluation (IS23-034–037/082).
5. **F1, required pulsed-wave sites and checklist item 4.** A learner can mistake an incomplete list for a complete specialist protocol. Restore bilateral pulmonary-vein sampling and put the unresolved arch/isthmus classification beside the list before using it for assessment (AS23-013/014 and the note to AS23-014).
