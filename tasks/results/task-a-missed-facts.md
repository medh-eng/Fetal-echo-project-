# Task A · Additional facts and the AS23-014 ruling

Page numbers are the printed journal pages. Each list uses the source ID in its heading.

## AS23-014 · What does ASE require at the arches?

**The prose includes “arches” in its minimum PW Doppler assessment. It does not individually name the transverse aortic arch or isthmus there.** Section III.G, *PW Doppler Ultrasound*, p. 689. Figure 10, p. 694 repeats the broad instruction.

**Table 6 marks the great-artery/arch row “Optional”.** That row names the transverse aortic segment and isthmus. A separate row makes PW Doppler of the distal ductus arteriosus at its aortic insertion required. Table 6, p. 693.

The discrepancy is between a general instruction and a site-specific table. ASE gives no explicit reconciliation in these passages. AS23-014 also drops the table’s great-artery wording from the optional list. For clinical adjudication, preserve both locators and the separate distal-ductus requirement. ## ASE-2023-FETALECHO · Additional facts

1. A normal early fetal echocardiogram still needs a second-trimester repeat. The early-examination table recommends reassessment at 18–20 weeks. III.A, *Timing*, p. 682, and Table 8, p. 696.
2. If the examiner cannot obtain diagnostic images after adjusting the scan, the report should specify the limitation. A repeat examination may be offered. III.D, *Image Optimization*, p. 684.
3. In the initial survey, look for fluid around the heart, in the pleural spaces and in the abdomen, and record whether it is present. III.C, *Examination Technique*, p. 684.
4. A complete fetal echocardiogram includes all four valve annuli. Table 4 specifies early diastole for the mitral and tricuspid measurements (p. 690). For the aortic and pulmonary annuli, III.F (p. 689) and the Table 4 footnote (p. 692) specify systole, while the Table 4 measurement row allows end-diastole or early systole, with the valve open or closed (p. 691).
5. The bicaval view can show an interrupted inferior vena cava and establish whether the two caval veins lie on the same side. III.E, *Bicaval view*, p. 689.
6. For early fetal echocardiography, monitor the soft-tissue thermal index before 10 weeks and the bone thermal index from 10 weeks onward. Table 8, *Imaging*, p. 696.
7. In suspected fetal supraventricular tachycardia, fused mitral inflow waves can obscure atrial timing. Table 9 advises against using the mitral–aortic flow recording for this assessment. IV.A, Table 9, *Tachycardia/SVT*, p. 698.

## ISUOG-2023-SCREEN · Additional facts

1. Many cardiac structures can still be assessed satisfactorily after 22 weeks. *Gestational age*, p. 789.
2. Recurrent fetal heart-rate decelerations in the third trimester can reflect fetal hypoxia. *Four-chamber view*, p. 792.
3. Limited lateral resolution makes confirmation of a small ventricular septal defect particularly difficult when fetal size or position is unfavourable. *Four-chamber view*, p. 793.
4. On the normal upper mediastinal views, the thymus appears as a relatively dark area between the great vessels and the front of the chest. Figures 7 and 9, pp. 795–796.
5. During first-trimester screening, adjust the balance between colour and greyscale to favour the flow signal in the small cardiac structures. *Early fetal cardiac screening*, p. 798.
6. A posterior fetal spine is the preferred orientation for demonstrating inflow into both ventricles in the early four-chamber view. *Early fetal cardiac screening*, item 2, p. 798.
7. Figure 10 illustrates early four-chamber colour assessment in diastole and the colour three-vessel-and-trachea view in systole. Figure 10 caption, p. 798.
